## 7. Conclusion

<b>A.1</b> The collocations attested with <span dir=”rtl”>אָלָה</span> and the varied translations exhibited by the Versions are indications of the polysemous nature of this noun, which is further confirmed by the contexts in which it is found.  
<b>A.2</b> <span dir=”rtl”>אָלָה</span> denotes a covenant, both that enacted between God and man, and that between nation and nation. Related to this is the meaning for <span dir=”rtl”>אָלָה</span> of an ‘agreement’, most frequently in legal contexts.  
<b>A.3</b> <span dir=”rtl”>אָלָה</span> may denote the ‘curses’ that are uttered in the concluding of a treaty, as well as the destructive force brought upon those who break an agreement.  
<b>A.4</b> As an extension of the meaning in A.3, <span dir=”rtl”>אָלָה</span> may denote a person who is subject to punishment brought about as a result of breaking an agreement (an anathema).
